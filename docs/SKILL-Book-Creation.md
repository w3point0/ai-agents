# Paperback Novel Polish & Review PDF Skill

## Purpose
Transform a rough story draft, concept treatment, synopsis, screenplay-like prose, or early novella manuscript into a **commercial paperback-style reading draft** suitable for review, print planning, and later editorial refinement.

This skill is optimized for work like:
- softcover / mass-market / newsstand-style fiction
- first-edition review drafts
- sci-fi, speculative, thriller, supernatural, and literary-commercial crossover fiction
- manuscripts that currently read too much like a pitch, outline, trailer, or treatment
- stories that need a **soft narrator voice** to add clarity, atmosphere, and continuity

The goal is **not** to overwrite the author's voice. The goal is to reshape the manuscript so it reads like a real novel page: denser paragraphs, smoother dialogue, more emotional anchoring, clearer chapter rhythm, and better readability for a general audience.

---

## When to Use
Use this skill when the user asks for any of the following:
- convert my draft into a paperback-style novel
- make this read like a real book
- add a narrator voice
- commercialize the prose
- make this feel like a mass-market paperback
- prepare a review manuscript in PDF format
- polish chapters for first-edition print review
- turn synopsis-like prose into true narrative prose
- make dialogue feel natural and shelf-ready

Use especially when the source text has one or more of these traits:
- too many isolated dramatic one-line paragraphs
- over-compressed exposition
- high-concept density without enough reader guidance
- dialogue that sounds quotable but not lived-in
- too much “statement prose” and not enough scene texture
- large visual gaps on the page
- markdown/document feel instead of book feel
- narrative complexity that would benefit from a quiet, clarifying narrator

---

## Core Outcome
Produce a **reviewable paperback-style manuscript** with:
- proper front matter
- prologue if useful
- numbered chapters or restrained chapter titles
- denser, natural paragraphs
- normalized dialogue flow
- a soft narrator voice used sparingly
- emotionally anchored scene transitions
- reduced slogan-density
- cleaner pacing for general readers
- export to PDF for reading and markup
- optional DOCX for future editing

## Source Material Accepted
This skill can work from:
- markdown draft
- notes or outline
- partial manuscript
- synopsis / treatment
- concept deck text
- chapter fragments
- text pasted directly into chat
- uploaded files

If the source is fragmentary, infer a reasonable commercial chapter structure while preserving the original plot spine.

---

## Editorial Philosophy
This skill follows these rules:

1. **Preserve the story spine.**
   Keep the main plot, reveals, lore, and emotional architecture intact unless the user explicitly asks for structural changes.

2. **Do not flatten the author.**
   Commercial readability should increase, but distinctive imagery and intellectual weight should remain.

3. **Clarity over performance.**
   Replace theatrical overstatement with grounded, flowing prose.

4. **Narrator as ghost, not lecturer.**
   The narrator should lightly reframe, clarify, and haunt; never dominate.

5. **Read like a bookstore novel page.**
   The result should feel readable in print, not like a screenplay, prompt, pitch, or lore memo.

6. **A general audience must be able to follow it.**
   High-concept content is allowed, but it must be carried through scene, context, and narrator support.

## The Narrator Layer
The narrator is a key differentiator in this workflow.

### Narrator Role
The narrator should:
- quietly restate what matters after complex events
- guide the reader through tonal or conceptual jumps
- add a faint haunted or remembered quality
- underline irony, dread, tenderness, or inevitability
- help the reader emotionally process reveals

### Narrator Should NOT
- explain everything
- sound academic
- interrupt every scene
- become preachy
- repeat plot mechanics the reader already understands
- replace scene-writing with commentary

### Ideal Narrator Tone
- soft
- observant
- restrained
- slightly ghostly
- emotionally intelligent
- memorable, but never showy

### Example Narrator Moves
- “At the time, she still believed this could be explained in ordinary terms.”
- “Later, the reports would make the room sound smaller than it had been.”
- “People often mistake reasonableness for safety.”
- “Some decisions feel practical until the door closes behind them.”

### Recommended Balance
A good working ratio is:
- 70% close scene prose
- 20% soft narrator support
- 10% mythic or atmospheric lift

Adjust by genre.

## Page-Level Commercialization Rules
To achieve true paperback style, apply these transformations:

### 1. Paragraph Density
Convert fragments and isolated emphasis lines into real paragraphs.

#### Before
- one-line drama beats
- excessive white space
- trailer-like pacing

#### After
- 3–7 sentence paragraphs
- scene information blended with reaction
- physical setting + action + thought integrated together

### 2. Dialogue Normalization
Keep strong lines, but surround them with:
- gesture
- interruption
- physical context
- hesitation
- reaction
- consequence

Dialogue should sound spoken by people inside a room, not like a chain of taglines.

### 3. Exposition Smoothing
When the story contains technical, alien, political, or lore-heavy concepts:
- spread them across movement and tension
- use the narrator to soften landings
- avoid stacking too many coined terms in one paragraph
- anchor every concept in a character’s need, fear, or decision

### 4. Emphasis Control
Use italics, all-caps, and visual emphasis sparingly.
Reserve strong visual emphasis for rare moments such as:
- system messages
- ritual commands
- catastrophic declarations
- chapter/section ornaments if appropriate

### 5. Chapter Pull
Every chapter should end with either:
- a reveal
- a decision
- a dread note
- a question
- a reversal
- a forward-pulling line

Avoid ending chapters on abstract thesis alone.

## Recommended Book Packaging
For a first-edition review manuscript, package like this:

1. Title page
2. Copyright page
3. “First Edition” line
4. Optional epigraph
5. Prologue (if earned)
6. Chapters in clean sequence
7. Optional “About the Author” placeholder

### Typical Front Matter Example
- Title
- Subtitle or series line if applicable
- Author name
- First Edition

### Chapter Style
Prefer one of these:
- `Prologue`, `1`, `2`, `3`...
- `Chapter One`, `Chapter Two`...
- restrained named chapters only if they truly help tone

For most commercial sci-fi/thriller work, simple numbering usually feels strongest.

## Workflow

### Step 1: Read the Source for Story Spine
Identify:
- protagonist
- major stakes
- governing threat
- reveal sequence
- emotional center
- ending mode
- recurring motifs

Write down the minimum story backbone before rewriting.

### Step 2: Identify Why It Does Not Yet Read Like a Novel
Look for:
- treatment-like compression
- excessive fragments
- abstract declarations
- too many quotable lines in a row
- weak interiority
- overly sparse page shape
- concept overload

### Step 3: Define the Commercial Reading Mode
Choose the target mode, such as:
- mass-market sci-fi thriller
- literary-commercial speculative
- supernatural suspense paperback
- philosophical thriller with mainstream readability

### Step 4: Build Chapter Architecture
If the original is loose, infer a chapter sequence from the existing events.
Do not overcomplicate. Use the simplest structure that carries momentum.

### Step 5: Rewrite in Close Narrative Prose
For each section:
- merge fragments into paragraphs
- thread dialogue into lived scene context
- give the protagonist perception priority
- preserve signature lines when earned
- cut repetition and pitch-language

### Step 6: Add Narrator Pass
On a second pass, add the narrator in strategic places:
- after reveals
- between scenes
- after exposition bursts
- near chapter endings
- when emotional clarity is needed

### Step 7: Polish for Readability
Check:
- paragraph rhythm
- dialogue tags and flow
- repeated phrasing
- chapter ending strength
- consistency of tense and viewpoint
- terminology load

### Step 8: Format for Review PDF
Create a clean reading manuscript:
- readable trim-like page size if practical
- serif body font
- centered chapter headings
- page numbers for body text
- modest margins
- no distracting design gimmicks

Optional: also export DOCX for line-editing.

## Style Rules

### Keep
- strong images
- key reveal lines
- emotional stakes
- distinct worldbuilding
- the author’s unusual energy when it serves the story

### Reduce
- excessive one-line dramatic beats
- manifesto-like prose inside scenes
- too much abstraction in a row
- repeated slogan constructions
- markdown-like visual pacing

### Avoid
- purple prose overload
- overexplaining lore
- making all characters sound equally wise
- turning the narrator into a lecturer
- flattening intellectual material into generic thriller language

---

## Heuristics for Commercial Novel Feel
A page is probably working when:
- it visually resembles a novel page rather than notes
- the eye moves naturally from paragraph to paragraph
- dialogue feels embodied
- the protagonist remains the emotional anchor
- the narrator is felt more than noticed
- a general reader can follow the story without losing the strangeness

A page is probably not working when:
- every paragraph sounds quotable
- scene geography is vague
- lore outruns feeling
- there is too much white space
- major lines sound like captions instead of thought or speech

---

## Special Guidance for Speculative / Sci-Fi Work
When dealing with advanced concepts, alien entities, doctrines, machine systems, or metaphysical governance:
- pair every abstract concept with a scene consequence
- reveal through confrontation when possible
- use the narrator to re-humanize the stakes
- resist introducing too many coined terms in a single burst
- keep the protagonist’s uncertainty legible

If there is a major nonhuman reveal, ensure:
- the scene is grounded first
- reactions are paced
- exposition follows shock, not vice versa
- the narrator helps the reader digest scale without draining mystery

---

## Print Review Considerations
This skill is for **review and refinement**, not final print lock.

A review PDF should be:
- comfortable to read on screen or tablet
- suitable for mark-up
- plain enough for multiple future editions
- close enough to paperback rhythm that the author can judge pacing honestly

Before final print, the author may still want:
- copyedit pass
- continuity pass
- trim-size decision
- typography adjustments
- widows/orphans cleanup
- print cover alignment
- ISBN/publishing metadata
